The State of LLM Progress:
Plateau, Paradigm Shift, or Both?

A multi-agent, fact-checked analysis of cost deflation, speed gains, intelligence growth, and 24-month forecasts — including a dedicated red-hat contrarian review.

Quick Summary — 60 Seconds

  • Inference costs are falling ~10× per year for equivalent capability. GPT-3-level performance dropped from $60/M tokens (2021) to $0.06/M tokens (2024) — a 1,000× reduction in 3 years.
  • Inference speed has improved 100–1,000× since 2020, driven by FlashAttention, vLLM, speculative decoding, and new hardware (Cerebras: 2,314 tok/s on Llama-70B; NVIDIA Blackwell: 40,000+ tok/s).
  • Intelligence gains have been dramatic but uneven. MMLU: 44%→92%. AIME math: 13%→97%. SWE-bench coding: 49%→81%. But many benchmarks are now saturated, and ARC-AGI-2 shows models still score <38% vs. humans at 100%.
  • Pre-training scaling has hit diminishing returns — this is near-consensus. But progress has shifted to inference-time compute (o1/o3), MoE architectures, and agentic workflows, which are delivering real capability gains.
  • The red-hat view is sobering: Training costs are growing 2.4×/year, hallucination remains structural (28–82%), the data wall approaches (exhaustion by 2026–2032), 95% of enterprise AI investments show zero ROI, and transformers have provable theoretical ceilings.
  • The next 24 months are decisive. $690B+ in 2026 hyperscaler capex bets on continued progress. If GPT-5, Gemini 4, and Claude 5 show major jumps, the thesis survives. If they plateau, a multi-trillion-dollar reckoning begins.

Scaling Laws — Are We Still on the Curve?

The original Kaplan et al. (2020) scaling laws predicted smooth power-law improvements with more compute. Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al., 2022) refined this, showing data matters as much as parameters. The current picture:

  • Pre-training scaling is hitting diminishing returns — near-consensus among researchers
  • Inference-time scaling (o1/o3 paradigm) has opened a new scaling dimension
  • The "Densing Law" (Nature Machine Intelligence, 2025) observes that capability per parameter is improving at a consistent rate
  • Small accuracy gains compound: 95%→97% single-step accuracy = 6× improvement on 100-step tasks

Section 5 — Devil's Advocate

Red Hat: The Uncomfortable Truths

A dedicated contrarian analysis to ensure this report isn't just cheerleading for AI progress.

1. Benchmark Saturation & Goodhart's Law

GSM8K, MMLU, HellaSwag, and HumanEval are all functionally saturated. Rising scores increasingly reflect optimization for benchmarks, not rising intelligence. The LMArena scandal revealed Meta tested 27 private LLM variants on Chatbot Arena before Llama 4, only publishing the top score. A study by Cohere, Stanford, MIT, and Ai2 found that even modest access to Arena data could boost scores by up to 112%.

Sources: The Register, TechCrunch

2. Training Costs Are Exploding

While inference drops, frontier training costs grow at 2.4×/year. GPT-4: $78M. Gemini Ultra: $191M. Heading toward $1B single runs. Only a handful of companies can afford this, concentrating AI development and "limiting the range of perspectives and approaches considered, especially from academia and broader society."

Source: Cottier et al., arXiv:2405.21015

3. Hallucination Is Structural, Not a Bug

Large-scale evaluation shows factual hallucination rates of 28–82% across major LLMs. The root cause is architectural: "training minimizes cross-entropy over token sequences, rewarding statistically probable continuations rather than per-instance factual accuracy." OpenAI's own September 2025 paper acknowledges that "next-token training objectives and common leaderboards reward confident guessing over calibrated uncertainty, so models learn to bluff."

Sources: arXiv LLM Hallucination Survey, Lakera

4. The Data Wall Is Approaching

Epoch AI estimates the entire corpus of high-quality public text is ~300 trillion tokens, projected to be fully utilized between 2026 and 2032. OpenAI researchers stated that in developing GPT-4.5, "a shortage of fresh data was more of a constraint than a lack of computing power." Synthetic data risks model collapse — a Nature paper (Shumailov et al.) showed models trained recursively on synthetic data degrade progressively.

Sources: Epoch AI, Nature (Shumailov et al.)

5. The ROI Crisis

An August 2025 MIT Media Lab report stated that "despite $30–40 billion in enterprise investment into GenAI, 95% of organizations are getting zero return." An NBER study (February 2026) found 90% of firms reported no AI impact on workplace productivity. The infrastructure-to-revenue gap: ~$400B in 2025 CapEx vs. ~$100B in actual AI revenue. OpenAI committed $1.4 trillion over 8 years with only $13B in revenue.

Sources: Fortune, Menlo Ventures

6. Transformers Have Provable Theoretical Limits

A peer-reviewed paper demonstrates that transformers are upper bounded by uniform TC0 circuit classes and are "fundamentally unable to solve NC1-complete tasks" for realistic parameter budgets. An ICLR 2025 paper found LLMs "struggle with proof search" and "strongly prefer heuristics." Additionally, 4.4% of all US energy now goes to data centers, with the IEA projecting demand to double to 945 TWh by 2030.

Sources: arXiv:2402.08164, IEA

Section 6

The Verdict: Answering the Critical Questions

Have we reached a plateau?

Partially Yes

Pre-training scaling has hit diminishing returns — this is near-consensus, acknowledged even by Ilya Sutskever. Many benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HellaSwag, HumanEval) are saturated. However, progress has not stopped; it has shifted paradigms. Inference-time compute (o1/o3), MoE architectures, reasoning RL, and agentic workflows represent new scaling dimensions delivering real gains. ARC-AGI-2 (best model: 37.6% vs. human 100%) shows we are nowhere near human-level general intelligence. The "plateau" is in one specific dimension of scaling — not in overall capability.

Can it continue to grow at the current rate?

Unlikely at the Same Rate

The ~10×/year inference cost deflation may slow as low-hanging optimizations (quantization, MoE) are exhausted. The data wall (300T token limit, 2026–2032) and energy constraints (4.4% of US electricity, heading higher) create real headwinds. Training costs growing at 2.4×/year are becoming unsustainable for all but the wealthiest organizations. However, the convergence of new hardware (Blackwell, Groq, Cerebras, TPU Ironwood), new architectures (hybrid SSM+transformer), and inference scaling suggest continued rapid progress on a somewhat different trajectory. Expect 3–5×/year cost improvement rather than 10×.

Is there going to be a new breakthrough that speeds things up?

Likely, But Uncertain

Several candidates are in active development:

  • Inference-time scaling (already proven): o1/o3/R1 show 10–80 percentage point gains on reasoning tasks
  • Hybrid architectures: Mamba + Transformer hybrids (AI21 Jamba) show promising linear-scaling with context length
  • World models: Fei-Fei Li's World Labs and LeCun's AMI Labs ($1.03B) pursuing fundamentally different approaches
  • Hardware leaps: Cerebras WSE-3 (7,000× more memory bandwidth than H100), NVIDIA Blackwell, Google Ironwood TPU
  • Agentic systems: Multi-agent pipelines showing emergent capabilities beyond individual model improvements

The red-hat view: Transformers have provable theoretical limits, hallucination appears structural, and no alternative architecture has yet demonstrated clear superiority at scale. Breakthroughs are possible but not guaranteed.

What can we expect in the coming 24 months?

High-Confidence Predictions

Based on the weight of evidence:

  • Inference costs will drop another 5–30× by early 2028, driven by Blackwell/Ironwood hardware and continued algorithmic gains
  • Reasoning models become standard — the o1/R1 paradigm will be table stakes, not a differentiator
  • Agentic AI goes mainstream — autonomous coding, research, and business process agents in production at scale. MIT Technology Review already named generative coding a 2026 breakthrough technology
  • Context windows reach 10M+ tokens routinely, enabling new workflows (entire codebases, full document collections)
  • Open-source parity continues — DeepSeek, Llama, Qwen will match proprietary models within months of release, commoditizing base capabilities
  • The ROI reckoning arrives — the $690B capex bet must show returns. Expect consolidation if GPT-5/Gemini 4/Claude 5 show only marginal gains
  • Regulation tightens — EU AI Act fully enforced, US executive orders, China's evolving framework. Compliance costs will be significant

The balanced view: We are not in a plateau — we are in a paradigm transition. The old scaling paradigm (bigger models + more data) is exhausting. New paradigms (inference compute, MoE, agents, hybrid architectures) are emerging and delivering real gains. But the rate of progress is becoming more uneven, the cost structure is shifting, and the gap between what AI can do on benchmarks versus reliable real-world deployment remains large. The next 24 months will likely bring impressive demo-worthy capabilities alongside persistent reliability challenges.
